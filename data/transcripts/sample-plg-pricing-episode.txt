Episode: Sample Episode - Pricing Product-Led Growth Products

Host: Today we're talking about pricing strategy for product-led growth (PLG)
companies. What's the biggest mistake you see teams make?

Guest: The most common mistake is pricing based on what competitors charge
instead of pricing based on the value delivered to a specific user segment.
Teams copy a competitor's tiered pricing page without understanding why that
structure exists for the competitor's business model.

Host: How should a team think about their first pricing page instead?

Guest: Start by identifying your core value metric -- the thing that scales
with the value a customer gets, like seats, usage volume, or API calls. Then
build tiers around natural upgrade triggers tied to that metric, not around
arbitrary feature bundling. A generous free tier is a growth lever, not a
cost center, as long as the value metric creates a believable upgrade path.

Host: When should a team introduce usage-based pricing?

Guest: Usage-based pricing works best when the product has a metered resource
that customers can directly observe and control, and when adoption is
land-and-expand rather than a single big purchase decision. It's risky to
introduce too early, before you understand your own cost structure, because
you can end up subsidizing your heaviest users.

Host: Any advice on communicating a pricing change to existing users?

Guest: Grandfather existing customers wherever possible, over-communicate the
"why" behind the change in terms of value delivered, and give at least 30-60
days notice. Pricing changes that feel like a bait-and-switch are one of the
fastest ways to spike churn and hurt word-of-mouth growth.
